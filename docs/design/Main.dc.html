3.2 Prevalence of cognitive impairment

Across the included studies, pooled prevalence of cognitive impairment among adults experiencing homelessness was substantially higher than in housed comparison populations, with estimates varying widely by assessment toolPrevalence. Heterogeneity was high and remained so after sensitivity analysis.

Most studies relied on brief screening instruments administered in shelter or drop-in settings, and few validated these instruments against a full neuropsychological batteryAssessment tools?. Where full batteries were used, deficits clustered in memory and executive function.

Several reviews noted that traumatic brain injury, alcohol use and prior psychiatric admission were each associated with impairment, although cross-sectional designs prevented any judgement about directionAetiology?.

The authors argue that unrecognised impairment may explain missed appointments and disengagement that services attribute to motivationService access. They call for routine screening linked to adapted care pathways.
